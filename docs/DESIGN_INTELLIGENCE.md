# Obsidian OS — Design Intelligence
## Canonical Internal Design Reference

> Status: Canonical design-intelligence reference
>
> Purpose: Convert accumulated external design research and internal design reasoning into actionable principles that Claude can use when designing Obsidian OS prospect preview websites.
>
> Core principle:
>
> **Evidence determines WHAT to claim. Design intelligence determines HOW to present it.**

---

# 1. PURPOSE

Obsidian OS exists to transform real local-business research and evidence into high-quality prospect preview websites.

The system must not merely generate technically competent websites.

The objective is to produce previews that feel:

- intentional
- premium
- editorial
- visually coherent
- industry-appropriate
- evidence-grounded
- differentiated when justified
- restrained rather than over-designed
- substantially better than a generic template

Design intelligence exists to determine HOW the available evidence should be presented.

It does NOT give permission to invent evidence.

---

# 2. SOURCE OF DESIGN INTELLIGENCE

The accumulated design research referenced during Obsidian OS development included:

- X / Twitter design research and examples
- Refero
- Supahero
- GetLayers
- UX Pro Max
- Claude design skills
- internal design-pattern research
- internal Obsidian OS design analysis
- observations from real generated prospect previews

These sources should be treated as research inputs.

Obsidian OS should NOT depend on these external sources at runtime.

The goal is to INTERNALIZE useful principles.

The production system should therefore use:

RESEARCH
→ INTERNAL DESIGN INTELLIGENCE
→ PROSPECT-SPECIFIC DESIGN REASONING
→ EXISTING DESIGN SYSTEM
→ RENDERED PREVIEW

Not:

RESEARCH
→ GENERIC AI OPINION
→ TEMPLATE

---

# 3. CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## 3.1 Evidence determines WHAT

Only real prospect evidence may determine factual claims.

Examples of evidence:

- business name
- location
- address
- phone
- hours
- real photographs
- real menu items
- real services
- verified business characteristics
- verified website content
- verified public information

Do not manufacture:

- testimonials
- awards
- credentials
- services
- menu items
- reviews
- business history
- claims of quality
- statistics
- certifications
- imagery
- customer quotes

When evidence is weak, the design should become MORE restrained, not more imaginative.

# 4. DESIGN INTELLIGENCE

Design intelligence determines HOW verified evidence should be presented.

Design intelligence can influence:

- composition
- hero treatment
- typography
- spacing
- visual hierarchy
- image treatment
- section rhythm
- CTA hierarchy
- navigation
- color roles
- narrative sequencing
- motion
- visual density
- editorial character
- industry-appropriate visual language

Design intelligence should be treated as a decision-making layer rather than decorative advice.

---

# 5. HIGH-END DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## 5.1 Intentionality Over Decoration

A premium site does not look premium because it contains more effects.

It looks premium because:

- hierarchy is intentional
- composition is intentional
- typography is intentional
- whitespace is intentional
- imagery is intentional
- CTA placement is intentional
- section transitions are intentional
- visual emphasis is intentional

Avoid adding decorative elements simply to make a page appear sophisticated.

Every major visual decision should have a reason.

---

# 6. COMPOSITION INTELLIGENCE

Composition is one of the primary mechanisms for making a site feel designed rather than templated.

Existing Obsidian OS renderer capabilities include:

- image-full-bleed
- split-media-text
- oversized-typographic
- editorial-typographic
- centered-cinematic
- offset-overlap

These existing capabilities should be reused.

Do not create a competing layout engine merely to create visual variety.

## 6.1 Composition Should Respond to Evidence

Consider:

- quantity of usable photography
- quality of photography
- type of business
- visual character of the business
- amount of available textual evidence
- strength of brand identity
- prominence of location
- prominence of products/services
- credibility evidence
- emotional character of the business

Strong photographic evidence can support photography-led composition.

Weak photographic evidence should not force photography-heavy design.

Strong textual identity can support typography-led composition.

Thin evidence should produce restrained composition rather than invented content.

---

# 7. HERO INTELLIGENCE

The hero should establish the site's visual and informational character immediately.

Existing hero archetypes:

1. Image Full Bleed
2. Split Media + Text
3. Oversized Typographic
4. Editorial Typographic
5. Centered Cinematic
6. Offset Overlap

Hero selection should be evidence-driven and industry-aware.

The hero should establish:

- business identity
- strongest available message
- visual character
- primary hierarchy
- appropriate CTA relationship

Avoid making every business use the same hero structure.

---

# 8. INDUSTRY CONSTRAINTS

Industry matters.

The system should not blindly apply the same visual treatment to every business.

Industry can influence:

- appropriate hero archetype
- composition
- density
- typography character
- imagery emphasis
- CTA prominence
- credibility emphasis
- narrative order

However:

**Industry should constrain design choices, not dictate a single universal template.**

The previous industry audit found that some industries legitimately converge on similar design archetypes.

Do not manufacture differentiation where evidence does not justify it.

Real estate demonstrated that certain industries may have a narrower set of appropriate compositions.

Preserve the existing industry-constrained hero-pattern behavior unless new evidence proves it inadequate.

---

# 9. TYPOGRAPHY INTELLIGENCE

Typography should establish visual character, not merely display text.

Consider:

- business personality
- industry
- evidence density
- hero strength
- editorial character
- sophistication
- warmth vs authority
- display-led vs information-led presentation

Typography should establish:

- display hierarchy
- supporting hierarchy
- body readability
- CTA prominence
- navigation hierarchy
- section hierarchy

Existing adaptive typography reasoning is part of the production system.

Do not replace it with a competing typography system.

Typography reasoning must result in an observable production difference.

---

# 10. SPACING AND WHITESPACE

Premium design often depends as much on what is NOT crowded as what is present.

Spacing should create:

- hierarchy
- breathing room
- rhythm
- emphasis
- separation
- visual confidence

Spacing should not simply mean "make everything bigger."

Existing adaptive spacing and narrative spacing behavior should remain deterministic.

Informational sections may require tighter rhythm.

Trust-building, credibility, contact, and footer areas may deserve more breathing room.

Spacing should communicate importance.

---

# 11. VISUAL RHYTHM

Avoid stacking identical sections with identical density.

A premium page should have rhythm.

Rhythm can be created through changes in:

- section height
- whitespace
- image scale
- text density
- typography scale
- alignment
- composition
- visual emphasis

The goal is not randomness.

The goal is controlled variation.

---

# 12. IMAGE INTELLIGENCE

Real photography is evidence.

When strong photography exists, it should be treated as a major design asset.

Do not treat every image as an identical card in a generic gallery.

Image treatment can consider:

- prominence
- cropping
- aspect ratio
- visual scale
- placement
- grouping
- sequencing
- relationship to text
- relationship to hero composition

Photography should contribute to art direction.

Do not create new stock-image or AI-image systems merely to make a preview appear more impressive.

If the prospect has weak photographic evidence, design around that limitation.

---

# 13. VISUAL HIERARCHY

A high-end site should make it obvious:

1. What is this?
2. Why should I care?
3. What should I look at?
4. What should I do next?

The page should not give every element equal visual weight.

Establish:

- primary focal point
- secondary supporting elements
- tertiary information

Hierarchy should exist at:

- page level
- hero level
- section level
- typography level
- CTA level
- image level

---

# 14. CTA INTELLIGENCE

The primary CTA should have clear hierarchy.

Avoid:

- multiple competing primary CTAs
- excessive button treatment
- every link looking like a CTA
- CTA prominence disconnected from the business evidence

The CTA should support the site's narrative and business purpose.

CTA treatment should be consistent with the site's visual character.

Existing CTA and composition-variant logic should be preserved.

**Disclosed gap.** CTA visual treatment (filled/outline/text-link, nav style) is wired sitewide and deterministic. CTA copy is not: DesignMemory's stated primary/secondary CTA text is real design-intelligence output, but the rendered hero and contact CTA labels are currently fixed strings and do not read it. This is a known, disclosed limitation, not solved behavior — do not assume CTA copy is production-wired without checking.

---

# 15. NAVIGATION INTELLIGENCE

Navigation should reflect the visual character of the site.

Possible behavior can include:

- linked navigation
- CTA-prominent navigation
- business information
- phone prominence where appropriate

Navigation should remain restrained.

Do not add navigation elements merely because a typical template contains them.

---

# 16. COLOR INTELLIGENCE

Color should communicate visual identity.

Natural-language color reasoning may describe a prospect in terms such as:

- terracotta
- olive
- cream
- charcoal
- aged wood
- amber
- brass

These descriptions should be resolved into existing Obsidian OS palette/theme tokens.

Color reasoning must then assign semantic roles correctly.

Semantic roles include:

- page background
- hero background
- footer/supporting background
- foreground/text
- accent
- secondary/supporting color

Color should NOT simply be assigned based on which color happens to be numerically lightest or darkest.

The semantic meaning of the palette matters.

Contrast safety is mandatory.

Do not sacrifice readability for visual character.

---

# 17. SEMANTIC COLOR ROLE PRINCIPLE

A palette is not enough.

The system must understand what each color is FOR.

For example:

A palette containing:

- cream
- charcoal
- terracotta
- olive

should not automatically make terracotta the page background simply because the algorithm chooses it as a candidate.

The system should reason about:

- calm/base background
- strongest visual surface
- text/foreground
- accent
- secondary/supporting color

Color roles must be contrast-safe.

Existing trusted WCAG/contrast logic should be reused rather than duplicated.

---

# 18. NARRATIVE INTELLIGENCE

The established narrative arc is:

**ESTABLISH → REVEAL → DEMONSTRATE → VALIDATE → DEEPEN → CONVERT**

This is an important design principle.

It describes how the visitor should progressively understand the business.

## ESTABLISH

Immediately establish:

- who the business is
- what it is
- where it is
- its strongest identity

## REVEAL

Introduce additional character, offerings, visual identity, or useful information.

## DEMONSTRATE

Show real evidence:

- photography
- menu
- services
- products
- locations
- other verified material

## VALIDATE

Use real credibility evidence when available.

Do not invent credibility.

## DEEPEN

Reinforce trust and credibility immediately before the ask — the bridge between validation and conversion.

## CONVERT

Make the appropriate next action clear.

Examples may include:

- contact
- call
- visit
- booking
- inquiry

---

# 19. NARRATIVE VS SPACING

Current Obsidian OS narrative logic materially affects production spacing.

That behavior is valid.

Trust-building and conversion-oriented portions may receive more breathing room than informational sections.

However, narrative intelligence should NOT automatically be assumed to require section reordering.

A future change should only be made if real regenerated previews demonstrate that:

- the narrative logic is correct,
- but the physical page structure fails to communicate that narrative.

Do not implement narrative reordering merely because it sounds more sophisticated.

---

# 20. MOTION INTELLIGENCE

Motion should support hierarchy and premium feel.

Motion should NOT:

- overwhelm the content
- become a gimmick
- distract from evidence
- make every element animate
- become a substitute for good composition

Existing motion budget and shader-hero behavior should be preserved.

Motion should remain restrained and purposeful.

---

# 21. SHADER / SPECIAL VISUAL TREATMENT

The shader hero is an existing production capability.

It should be treated as a special visual treatment rather than the default solution.

Use only when it fits the prospect's visual character.

It must remain:

- restrained
- performant
- contrast-safe
- compatible with reduced-motion behavior
- subordinate to actual business evidence

Do not expand the shader system simply to add visual novelty.

---

# 22. EDITORIAL DESIGN

Editorial quality comes from:

- strong hierarchy
- intentional whitespace
- meaningful scale differences
- controlled asymmetry
- image/text relationships
- pacing
- restrained typography
- visual confidence

Editorial does NOT mean:

- random asymmetry
- oversized everything
- excessive whitespace
- decorative complexity
- forced unconventional layouts

---

# 23. VISUAL DENSITY

Different businesses require different densities.

A restaurant with extensive photography may support:

- larger images
- more visual storytelling
- tighter transitions between image-driven sections

A professional service business may require:

- stronger credibility
- clearer hierarchy
- more restrained visual density
- greater emphasis on trust

Density should respond to evidence.

---

# 24. DIFFERENTIATION

Obsidian OS should not produce five completely unrelated websites simply for the sake of variety.

Differentiation should be earned.

Meaningful differences may come from:

- photography
- industry
- typography
- palette
- composition
- visual identity
- evidence density
- narrative emphasis
- CTA hierarchy
- spacing
- imagery
- motion restraint

The question is:

> Would this business naturally justify this visual treatment?

If yes, use it.

If no, do not force it.

---

# 25. DESIGN MEMORY

Persisted DesignMemory should be treated as meaningful design input.

When DesignMemory contains reasoning about:

- visual identity
- composition
- typography
- color
- spacing
- imagery
- narrative
- motion

the production system should determine whether that reasoning maps to an existing production capability.

DesignMemory must not become a dead-end storage location.

Where an existing renderer capability already exists, design reasoning should feed it.

---

# 26. PRODUCTION WIRING PRINCIPLE

The strongest test of design intelligence is:

> If the input changes, does the rendered preview change?

For every design-intelligence decision, establish:

INPUT
→ REASONING
→ DECISION
→ RENDERER
→ VISIBLE EFFECT

Example:

Business evidence:
Strong editorial photography

↓

Design reasoning:
Photography should be dominant

↓

Composition:
Image-led hero

↓

Renderer:
Image-full-bleed hero

↓

Visible effect:
Photography becomes the primary visual focal point.

If the final rendered site does not change, the intelligence is not actually wired into production.

# 27. EXISTING OBSIDIAN OS DESIGN FIXES

The following design fixes already exist and must be treated as production behavior.

## Fix #1 — Adaptive Typography

Design reasoning influences actual typography scale/weight.

This is deterministic and tested.

Do not replace it with another typography engine.

---

## Fix #2 — Composition Variants

Composition reasoning influences:

- navigation style
- CTA treatment
- footer treatment

Existing behavior is deterministic.

Do not create a competing composition system.

---

## Fix #3 — Industry-Constrained Hero Patterns

Industry constraints influence hero-pattern selection.

Existing industry research demonstrated that some industries legitimately converge on similar archetypes.

Preserve the existing behavior unless new evidence demonstrates a problem.

---

## Fix #4 — Adaptive Spacing

Spacing adapts based on design reasoning and page context.

Spacing is part of visual hierarchy and rhythm.

---

## Fix #5 — Narrative Arc Production Wiring

Narrative reasoning currently affects production spacing and breathing room.

Established narrative:

ESTABLISH
→ REVEAL
→ DEMONSTRATE
→ VALIDATE
→ DEEPEN
→ CONVERT

Do not assume this needs structural reordering without visual evidence.

---

## Fix #6 — Typography Reasoning

Natural-language typography reasoning is connected to production typography.

Its effect must remain observable and deterministic.

---

## Fix #7 — Color Resolution

Natural-language color reasoning is resolved into existing palette/theme tokens.

Do not replace this with arbitrary hex generation.

---

## Fix #8 — Semantic Color Roles + Contrast Safety

Resolved colors are assigned semantic roles.

Contrast safety is mandatory.

The system must distinguish:

- calm/base background
- strongest appropriate hero surface
- supporting/footer surface
- foreground/text
- semantic accent

Accent must not automatically become a background.

# 28. DESIGN DECISION FRAMEWORK

When designing a prospect, Claude should reason in this order:

## STEP 1 — Evidence

What do we actually know?

## STEP 2 — Visual Evidence

What real photography, branding, colors, typography, or visual material exists?

## STEP 3 — Business Character

What visual character does the evidence support?

Examples:

- warm
- refined
- editorial
- energetic
- minimal
- traditional
- modern
- authoritative
- playful

Do not invent characteristics without evidence.

## STEP 4 — Industry Constraints

What design patterns are appropriate for this industry?

## STEP 5 — Design Intelligence

Which researched principles are relevant?

## STEP 6 — Composition

Which existing composition capability best expresses the evidence?

## STEP 7 — Typography

Which existing typography behavior supports the identity?

## STEP 8 — Color

Which semantic palette roles best communicate the identity while preserving contrast?

## STEP 9 — Spacing

What rhythm and breathing room communicate hierarchy?

## STEP 10 — Imagery

How should actual photography be treated?

## STEP 11 — Narrative

How should the visitor move through the evidence?

## STEP 12 — CTA

What is the appropriate primary action?

## STEP 13 — Motion

Would restrained motion improve the experience?

## STEP 14 — Validation

Does the resulting production output visibly reflect the reasoning?

# 29. WHAT CLAUDE SHOULD NOT DO

Do NOT:

- copy a website from X
- recreate a Refero example literally
- reproduce a Supahero layout literally
- treat inspiration as a template
- randomly vary layouts
- invent content to fill empty sections
- manufacture testimonials
- manufacture credibility
- fabricate services
- fabricate imagery
- add decorative complexity without reason
- create a new layout engine unnecessarily
- add runtime dependencies on external design-research sites
- use random visual variation
- generate multiple concepts simply for variety
- redesign the dashboard as part of website design work
- introduce new stock-image systems merely for visual appeal
- introduce GSAP or otherwise expand motion capabilities without a validated production need

# 30. HIGH-END QUALITY TEST

A preview should not be judged by:

"Does it contain all the normal website sections?"

It should be judged by:

### Identity

Does it immediately feel like THIS business?

### Hierarchy

Is it obvious what matters most?

### Composition

Does the page feel intentionally composed?

### Typography

Does type establish character and hierarchy?

### Spacing

Does whitespace create confidence and rhythm?

### Imagery

Are real images treated as meaningful visual assets?

### Color

Are colors assigned according to semantic roles?

### Narrative

Does the visitor understand the business progressively?

### CTA

Is the next action clear without becoming aggressive?

### Restraint

Does the design avoid unnecessary effects?

### Evidence

Could every factual claim be traced to real evidence?

### Differentiation

Does the design differ where the evidence justifies differentiation?

---

# 31. THE CENTRAL RULE

Obsidian OS should behave like an experienced creative director operating inside strict evidence boundaries.

It should ask:

> "Given what we know about this business, what is the strongest way to present that evidence?"

Not:

> "What website template should I use?"

And not:

> "How can I make this look flashy?"

---

# 32. RESEARCH INTERNALIZATION

External design research should continually be converted from:

OBSERVATION

into:

PRINCIPLE

into:

DESIGN RULE

into:

PRODUCTION DECISION

into:

VISIBLE RESULT

Example:

Observation:
Premium sites use deliberate whitespace.

↓

Principle:
Whitespace establishes hierarchy and perceived confidence.

↓

Design rule:
Important trust/conversion moments can receive greater breathing room.

↓

Production decision:
Adaptive/narrative spacing increases around credibility/contact/footer regions.

↓

Visible result:
The page feels less crowded and more intentional.

This is the standard for converting research into usable Obsidian OS intelligence.

# 33. FINAL DESIGN DIRECTIVE

The purpose of this document is NOT to make every Obsidian OS website look like an award-winning design gallery.

The purpose is to give Obsidian OS enough accumulated design intelligence to make better decisions automatically.

The desired result is:

**Evidence-grounded + visually intentional + industry-appropriate + premium + restrained + differentiated when justified.**

The design system should remain:

- deterministic
- explainable
- testable
- bounded
- evidence-driven
- compatible with unattended production

The highest standard is:

> **A prospect should look at the preview and feel that someone deliberately designed a website for THEIR business — not that a template was filled with their information.**

---

# 34. IMPLEMENTATION RULE

This document is a DESIGN INTELLIGENCE SOURCE.

It is not permission to immediately modify production code.

Before implementing any new design behavior:

1. Identify the existing renderer capability.
2. Identify the existing design-intelligence input.
3. Trace whether that input already reaches production.
4. Determine whether a new implementation is actually required.
5. Preserve all existing Fix #1–#8 behavior.
6. Use real prospect data where possible.
7. Add deterministic tests.
8. Demonstrate an actual rendered visual consequence.
9. Do not introduce redundant architecture.

When in doubt:

**Prefer wiring existing intelligence into existing capabilities over creating new capabilities.**

---

# 35. CANONICAL PRINCIPLE

## Evidence determines WHAT.
## Design intelligence determines HOW.
## The renderer determines WHAT THE PROSPECT ACTUALLY SEES.

If the design intelligence does not reach the renderer, it is not yet production intelligence.
